      How the percentages work

      GDP rules: a technical recession is two consecutive quarterly falls in real GDP. 0 falls = 0%, 1 fall = 50%, 2 falls = 100% (rule met). The same rule is applied separately to GDP per person.

      Unemployment (Sahm-style gap): the 3-month average unemployment rate compared with its lowest 3-month average over the previous 12 months. A gap of 0.50 percentage points = 100%. This is a US rule; an RBA-attributed 0.75 pp Australian variant is shown for comparison. Neither has been validated on Australian history here.

      Employment: the latest 3-month average compared with the previous 3 months. Falling = 100%, otherwise 0%.

      Signal index: 40% GDP rule, 30% unemployment gap, 15% GDP per person, 15% employment. It is a stress score, not the chance of recession. Weights are a judgement call and haven't been backtested.

      No trigger shown: the saving ratio, cash rate, terms of trade and employment-to-population ratio have no validated threshold. They're shown as context.

      Pending: insolvencies (ASIC) and mortgage arrears (APRA) need definition checks before they can be shown. No numbers are invented in the meantime.

      Economic data arrive with a delay and get revised. A warning means a stated condition is met. It doesn't guarantee a recession or predict when one will occur.

      Sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics; Reserve Bank of Australia. Calculations are this app's own. Not financial advice.
